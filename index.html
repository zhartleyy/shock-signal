Houthi Red Sea Attacks Resume, Container Capacity Stuck at 49%

↑ Freight Rate Spike

The Red Sea route to Europe is operating at just 49% of pre-crisis capacity after Houthi forces resumed attacks on commercial shipping, reversing gains made since the October 2025 ceasefire. Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, and Hapag-Lloyd have suspended Hormuz-area transits, forcing vessels onto longer Cape of Good Hope routings that consume more tonne-days and tighten global vessel supply. The dual chokepoint disruption has been described as the most severe Middle East supply chain event in modern history.

Mixed Signal

NOAA Confirms Below-Normal Atlantic Season on Strong El Niño

↓ Risk Normalization

NOAA's August 6 update maintains a below-normal Atlantic hurricane season forecast, citing a 90% chance of strong El Niño conditions through August-October that raise wind shear and suppress tropical development. Only two named storms, Arthur and Bertha, had formed by early August with no hurricanes yet, and every strong El Niño since 1950 has coincided with a below-normal season. Reduced landfall risk lowers expected catastrophe claims for insurers and eases supply risk for Gulf Coast crop and refining infrastructure.
