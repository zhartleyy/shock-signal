SIGNAL 04 · ENERGY
Semiconductors Supply Chain AI Chips

Qatar Helium Shock — Iran Strike on Ras Laffan Removes 30% of Global Semiconductor-Grade Supply

8
Conviction

Iranian strikes on QatarEnergy's Ras Laffan Industrial City in February knocked offline one of only two plants globally capable of producing semiconductor-grade helium, removing ~30% of worldwide supply in days. Helium plays irreplaceable roles in chip manufacturing: wafer cooling during etching, leak detection in precision equipment, and atmosphere control during HBM bonding. Fabs typically hold only a week's supply on-site. Cape of Good Hope rerouting adds 2+ weeks to helium delivery from Qatar to South Korea, creating a compounding supply crunch for Samsung and SK Hynix while US-based Micron and Intel fabs — sourcing from domestic and Algerian helium — maintain relatively stable supply. A global helium shortage is now accelerating AI chip reshoring to US-based foundries.

Step 01 — Scan

Global Event Monitoring

Step 02 — Trace

Causal Chain Mapping

Each event is traced through its mechanism: what gets scarcer or more abundant? Which companies have direct revenue or cost exposure? How does the shock propagate through supply chains, competitors, and end markets?

Step 03 — Score

Conviction Scoring

Signals are scored 1–10 based on three factors: directness of causal link (how many steps to impact), magnitude of revenue exposure, and how much is already priced in by the market. High scores require all three to align.

Signals are surfaced daily in this dashboard, updated before market open. Double beneficiaries — stocks positively impacted by two or more independent signals — are highlighted as potentially higher-conviction opportunities.
