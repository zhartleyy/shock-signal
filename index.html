IEA Sees Q4 Oil Surplus as OPEC+ Output Climbs

↓ Supply Normalization

The IEA's November report points to a fourth-quarter global oil surplus as Gulf supply recovers faster than demand, with global output up 2.3 million b/d in October to 105.4 million b/d and observed inventories up 52 million barrels. Brent settled at $95.84 on Friday, down about 2.5% for a second straight week, as OPEC+ output gains led by Saudi Arabia and rising US crude/fuel stocks outweigh lingering Hormuz risk. Falling crude feeds straight through to diesel and jet-fuel costs for carriers and truckers.

China's Rare-Earth Export Suspension Lapses, Reviving 0.1% Extraterritorial Rule

↓ Supply Deficit

China's one-year suspension of its toughest October 2025 rare-earth export controls expired November 10 with no renewal instrument published by MOFCOM, reviving a rule requiring Beijing's sign-off on any product made anywhere containing even 0.1% Chinese-origin rare-earth material by value. The IEA estimates roughly $6.5 trillion of Western EV, chip and defense output is exposed to the reinstated licensing regime, which covers magnet-grade elements like dysprosium, terbium and samarium. Buyers of specialized magnets for fighter jets and missile guidance systems face the most immediate scramble for alternative supply.

BIS Expands AI-Chip Licensing to Seven More Countries to Choke Compute Arbitrage

↓ Export Constraint

The Bureau of Industry and Security is finalizing a rule requiring licenses for the most advanced AI chips bound for seven additional countries, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, aimed at stopping Chinese developers from renting compute through overseas data centers. The move follows the November 10 reimposition of the Affiliates Rule and comes as lawmakers push the SAFE Chips Act and a 30-day congressional veto over export licenses. Tighter third-country routes push hyperscalers to concentrate new AI capacity onshore, reinforcing both chip-supplier order books and domestic power-equipment demand.

Flooded Lake Erie Salt Mine Pulls 700K-1.3M Tons Off Market Before Winter

↑ Supply Deficit

Water infiltration has shut Morton Salt's Fairport Harbor mine beneath Lake Erie, a facility that normally produces about 1.3 million tons of road salt a year and supplies six northeast Ohio counties. Mahoning County's engineer says the closure will leave the region with roughly a third of contracted salt supplies this winter, with replacement salt costing four to five times the contracted price if it can be found at all. The shortfall compounds an already-structural North American salt deficit after Cargill's 2021 Avery Island closure permanently removed about 2.5 million tons of annual capacity.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
