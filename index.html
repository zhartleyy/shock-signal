Copper Market Swings to Structural Deficit

↓ Supply Deficit

Analysts have revised the global refined copper market from an expected surplus to a structural deficit exceeding 150,000 tonnes, driven by mine disruptions including flooding at Kamoa-Kakula in the DRC and an accident at Chile's El Teniente, alongside a processing reagent cutoff tied to roughly 15% of global production. AI data centers and EVs, which require three to four times more copper than conventional vehicles, are structurally lifting demand even as new mine permitting in the US averages nearly 29 years. Goldman Sachs has said the copper deficit outside the US is now ten times larger than previously thought, rising to 640,000 tons.

Section 232 Tariff Ambiguity Splits COMEX-LME Copper Pricing

Mixed Signal

US policymakers have maintained deliberate ambiguity over a potential 10-15% Section 232 tariff on refined copper, a stance strategists say is designed to keep incentives for traders to maintain excess copper inventory onshore. The uncertainty has already pulled hundreds of thousands of tonnes of copper into US warehouses, swelling CME stocks while tightening availability in the rest of the world and widening the COMEX-LME spread by an estimated $0.30 to $0.80 per pound. That bifurcation benefits US-domiciled miners selling into the premium domestic market while squeezing downstream manufacturers reliant on global supply.
