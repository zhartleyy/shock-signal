🛢️ Energy / Geopolitics
Strait of Hormuz Remains Effectively Closed
A US-Israel strike campaign on Iran in late February (Operation Epic Fury) triggered retaliatory closures of the Strait of Hormuz — the chokepoint for ~35% of global seaborne crude and ~20% of global LNG. Only 191 vessels crossed in all of April. Brent averaged $117/bbl in April, global inventories drawing at 8.5M bbl/day. While a partial ceasefire is in place, shipping insurance withdrawal means the strait remains commercially closed. First resumption of meaningful flows expected late May / early June — but a full return to pre-conflict patterns is unlikely before late 2026.

🚢 Shipping / Commodities
VLCC Tanker Rates in Uncharted Territory — Index Tops $420K/Day
With Hormuz closed, crude must reroute around the Cape of Good Hope — adding 3–4 weeks and dramatically increasing tonne-miles. VLCC rates hit $420K/day (up from ~$30K/day pre-crisis). War-risk insurance premiums have priced most commercial tankers out of the strait entirely. Container freight from Asia to Europe also surged 15% week-on-week. Carriers blanked 10–15% of sailings to preserve GRI gains.

🔵 Energy / LNG
European LNG Supply Crunch — EU Gas Storage at 36% Capacity
Qatar, which supplies ~20% of global LNG, had its Hormuz exports effectively cut off in early March. EU gas storage sits at just 36% of capacity — well below the 2022 levels during the Russia-Ukraine energy crisis. Summer prices are consistently above winter prices, eliminating the incentive to store. US LNG exporters are filling the gap, with spot cargoes commanding record premia. Norwegian pipeline gas surged 40% on Troll field maintenance resolution. Europe faces an acute winter 2026 risk if storage isn't rebuilt.
