Section 338 Tariff Hits Canadian Goods; Ottawa Readies Retaliation

↓ Trade Flow Disruption

A 50% Section 338 tariff on a broad range of Canadian goods, including dairy, motor vehicles and alcoholic beverages, took effect August 22 after a brief suspension, applying even to USMCA-qualified goods based on country of origin. Canada has confirmed retaliatory tariffs of 15%, 25% or 50% on over 700 US-origin products, set to take effect September 8. British Columbia manufacturers report roughly 20% of sales tied to the now-disrupted US market, with federal relief programs only partially offsetting losses.

Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs Hit Imports From 60 Countries

↑ Cost-Push Inflation

USTR enacted a 10-12.5% ad valorem duty on goods from 60 countries found to have failed to stop forced-labor production, with a 25-state coalition led by Oregon, Arizona and California now suing at the Court of International Trade over the rushed investigation process. The tariff layers onto existing global 10% duties, raising landed costs for import-dependent retailers just ahead of the holiday ordering season. Low-margin discount retailers sourcing heavily from Southeast Asian manufacturing hubs face the sharpest cost pass-through pressure.

100% Pharma Tariff Squeezes Non-US Drugmakers

↑ Cost Barrier

A 100% tariff on patented pharmaceutical products and ingredients became effective July 31 for large companies, with smaller firms facing a September 29 deadline, unless companies secure MFN pricing agreements with HHS or onshoring deals with Commerce that reduce the rate to 0-20%. Companies without domestic production face a steep cost cliff on imported patented drugs sold into the US market.

RASA Bill Threatens Southeast Asia Neocloud Remote-Access Loophole

↓ Regulatory Constraint

US officials are investigating how Chinese AI firms including Moonshot AI, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have accessed restricted Nvidia GB300 chips by renting compute through data centers in Thailand, Malaysia and Japan rather than importing hardware directly. The proposed Remote Access Security Act, which passed the House in January but awaits Senate action, would extend export controls to cloud-based access of critical hardware. Passage would force compliance reviews on any cloud provider or fund with Southeast Asian neocloud exposure serving Chinese customers.

Hurricane Season Peak RiskClimate / Logistics

Atlantic Hurricane Season Hits Peak Window Amid Tight Port Capacity

↑ Disruption Risk

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season has entered its statistical peak between mid-August and mid-October, with forecasters expecting a below-normal 3-6 storm count but the freight market carrying less buffer capacity than average. A single Category 3 landfall at Houston, Savannah or New Orleans could close a terminal for 3-14 days, with vessel-queue clearance adding another 5-7 days, producing a total cargo delay of up to two weeks. Historical precedent includes Hurricane Ida's nine-day closure of New Orleans in 2021 and Harvey's 88% collapse in Houston trucking activity in 2017, which sent spot rates from roughly $2.40 to $8-10 per mile.

Diesel-Crude Divergence Pushes Refining Margins to Record Highs

↑ Margin Expansion

Atlantic Basin refining margins hit all-time highs in July as diesel, jet fuel and gasoline crack spreads surged on supply shortfalls and depleted stocks even as crude prices softened. The IEA cut its second-half 2026 global demand forecast by roughly 550,000 b/d, citing continued Hormuz-related disruption to product availability, while noting global oil demand is now expected to decline by an average 1.6 million b/d this year. Diesel and crude prices, which normally move together, have diverged as reduced refining throughput tightens a market where demand is hard to cut.
