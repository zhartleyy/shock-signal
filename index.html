Hormuz Escalation & Oil Supply ShockShipping / Insurance

War-Risk Insurance for Hormuz Transits Hits 10% of Hull Value

↑ Cost Inflation

Marine war-risk premiums for vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz have surged to between 7.5% and 10% of a ship's hull value, roughly 30-40 times pre-war levels near 0.25%, as underwriters grow reluctant to offer coverage amid intensifying tanker attacks. A $100 million tanker now faces war-risk premiums running into the millions of dollars per voyage, effectively rationing willing capacity and pushing up freight rates for ships still willing to sail. The Bab al-Mandeb strait is seeing a parallel premium spike, compounding global freight cost inflation.

US-Canada Tariff RetaliationRegulatory / Trade

Canada's $27.6 Billion Counter-Tariffs Take Effect September 8

↑ Trade Barrier

Canada has confirmed retaliatory tariffs of 15%, 25% or 50% on more than 700 US-origin products worth $27.6 billion, effective 12:01 a.m. September 8, following the collapse of trade talks with Washington. Steel and aluminum counter-tariffs specifically double from 25% to 50% to match the new US Section 232 rate, while new duties hit dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, plastics and electronics. The move protects domestic Canadian steelmakers while raising costs for US exporters reliant on the Canadian market.

Russia's Refining CollapseEnergy / Geopolitical

Ukrainian Drone Strikes Cripple Russian Refining Capacity

↓ Supply Deficit

Ukraine's deep-strike campaign has hit the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat complex, which processes up to 74 million barrels of oil annually, along with the Orsk refinery, whose governor said repairs to sanctioned foreign equipment could take up to six months. The onslaught has pushed Russian refinery runs to multi-year lows and triggered fuel rationing or shortages across more than 90% of Russian regions, forcing Moscow to restrict exports. Western refiners with spare distillate capacity are absorbing the resulting global gap in diesel and gasoline supply.

OPEC+ Completes Final 188,000 bpd Unwind, Holds Quotas Steady

↑ Supply Normalization

OPEC+ approved a final 188,000 bpd production increase for September, completing the phased rollback of the 1.65 million bpd of voluntary cuts first introduced in 2023, while signaling quotas will likely hold steady through year-end. A separate layer of roughly 2 million bpd in older cuts remains in place through the end of 2026, and delegates note the increases have had limited real-world market impact because war-related export disruptions from the Gulf and Russia continue to constrain actual flows. Analysts warn that an eventual easing of the Iran conflict could unlock a supply surplus as Gulf producers restore full output.
