Tankers Struck in Strait of Hormuz as US-Iran War Reignites

↑ Supply Shock

A Saudi-flagged and a South Korean-owned crude tanker were both struck by projectiles within minutes of each other transiting the Strait of Hormuz Monday night, days after US forces hit Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island that were preparing sea mines. Iran retaliated by firing ballistic missiles at US bases in Jordan, and Trump has vowed to hit back hard. The chokepoint normally carries roughly 20% of global seaborne oil and LNG shipments, and transits have collapsed more than 80% since the war began February 28, pushing Brent to $91/bbl and WTI above $86/bbl.

Tanker and LNG Freight Rates Hit Records as Hormuz Traffic Collapses

↑ Rate Spike

LR2 tanker rates from the Middle East Gulf to Japan hit a record $107.72 a ton on August 27, while the Atlantic Basin LNG freight benchmark Spark30 jumped $100,000 a day in a single session, the largest one-day move on record, as Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG terminal sits offline. Dry-bulk transits through the strait are down 91% with roughly 280 bulkers trapped in the region, tightening vessel supply across every shipping segment. Owners of tankers and LNG carriers are capturing windfall charter rates while cargo owners scramble for alternative tonnage.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
