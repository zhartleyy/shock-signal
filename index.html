Brent Crashes Toward $95 as Iran-US Peace Deal Nears

↓ Risk Premium Unwind

Brent crude fell roughly 18% over the week to around $94-96/bbl, including a near-9% single-session plunge, after Washington and Tehran signaled progress on a deal to end the eight-month war, with Iran offering to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if terms are met. Hormuz shipments are recovering toward prewar levels even as tankers reportedly caught fire in the strait during continued skirmishes. Markets are pricing out the geopolitical risk premium that had pushed Brent above $117 in late October.

Coordinated Refrigeration Hack Hits Supermarkets in 9 EU Nations

↓ Supply Disruption

Suspected cyberattacks disrupted refrigeration systems at supermarkets across at least nine European countries on November 2, including Belgium, Estonia, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal and Sweden. Carrefour reported roughly 15 of its 700 Belgian stores hit and Ireland's SuperValu saw 13 stores affected, with a Belgian butcher chain alone losing an estimated €15,000 of meat. The EU's food commissioner called it a deliberate attempt to disrupt the bloc's food supply chain, coming just three weeks after a similar attack hit hundreds of French grocery stores.

16-Month Vincent Thomas Bridge Closure Compounds LA/Long Beach Strike Risk

↓ Landside Capacity Constraint

Caltrans began a roughly 16-month, $750 million closure of the Vincent Thomas Bridge on November 7, a span connecting San Pedro to Terminal Island that normally carries up to 53,000 vehicles daily. While only about 8% of port-complex drayage traffic uses the bridge and terminals are expected to stay open, the closure compounds separate strike threats from roughly 250 ILWU Local 26 watchmen at LA/Long Beach, raising the risk of demurrage and turn-time increases during peak season.

600,000 Nvidia H200 Chips Stranded in Hong Kong as Beijing Withholds Approval

↓ Supply Chain Blockage

About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced H200 AI chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to mainland China as Beijing withholds approval for large-scale imports despite strong demand from Chinese technology firms. With authorized supply constrained, buyers have turned to intermediaries, pushing an eight-card H200 server's gray-market price above $900,000, roughly three times its level a year earlier, while Blackwell-based servers fetch more than $20 million apiece.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology
STEP 01 🔍

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

STEP 02 🔗

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

STEP 04 📬

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
