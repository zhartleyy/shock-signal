Airlines and consumer-facing logistics companies benefit from lower fuel costs; upstream oil producers and tanker operators that boomed during the conflict face sudden margin compression.

China Rare Earth Controls Still Biting — Yttrium Exports to US Down 95%, November Deadline Looms
China's April 2025 heavy rare earth export restrictions — originally framed as retaliation for US tariffs — continue to throttle supply chains. US yttrium imports fell from 333 metric tons (pre-controls) to just 17 metric tons, a 95% collapse. Aerospace manufacturers report active shortages of yttrium-stabilized coatings for turbine blades. A suspension of October controls expires November 10, 2026 — the IEA estimates $6.5 trillion in annual economic activity would be at risk from full reimplementation. The Trump-Xi summit produced vague promises on rare earth access but no hard agreement.
