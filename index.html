Hurricane Isaias Shuts In 25% of Gulf of Mexico Oil Output

↓ Supply Disruption

Shell and Chevron have evacuated personnel and shut in production at nine offshore platforms including Mars, Olympus, Ursa, Vito and Appomattox as Hurricane Isaias, the first Atlantic hurricane of 2026, bears down on the northern Gulf Coast. About 511,600 barrels a day, roughly 25% of regional output, and 16% of Gulf natural gas production are already offline ahead of a Friday-night landfall near the Florida-Alabama border. The storm arrives with refiners running above 90% utilization and product inventories at decades-long lows, leaving almost no buffer to absorb the hit.

Gasoline and Diesel Inventories Fall to Multi-Decade Lows

↓ Supply Deficit

US gasoline stockpiles have fallen to their lowest seasonal level since 2012, while distillate inventories sit at their lowest level for early October since record-keeping began in the early 1980s. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve has been drawn to about 283 million barrels, the least since 1982, leaving policymakers almost no buffer as Isaias threatens two to three weeks of refinery downtime in a worst-case scenario. Analysts warn a prolonged outage would collide with fuel markets already strained by the Iran war's hit to global refining capacity.

Chip Export Policy FluxTechnology / Trade Policy

Nvidia H200 Policy Reversal Reshapes US-China Chip Trade

Mixed Signal

After banning, then unbanning the H200 AI chip for China and layering on a 25% tariff, the Trump administration has created what experts call a contradictory licensing framework for advanced semiconductors. TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix each had to secure new annual export licenses from the Commerce Department starting January 1, 2026, replacing the automatic Validated End-User exemptions that expired at the end of 2025. The practical impact so far has been minimal, with fabs running and supply chains intact, but the shift to annual renewals gives Washington a yearly lever over all three companies' China operations.
