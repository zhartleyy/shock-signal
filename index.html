Copper's Worst Single-Day Crash in 8 Months Erases $50B From Miners

↓ Demand Destruction

Copper fell nearly 5% to $5.69 per pound on November 18, its worst single-session collapse in eight months, wiping out roughly $50 billion in miner market value in one day and a combined $141 billion across eleven major copper-exposed producers over the past month. Freeport-McMoRan fell 20% over the month including a 6.8% single-day drop on November 16, while Ivanhoe Mines dropped 30% and Southern Copper fell 22%. Notably, China's physical copper market tightened even as futures cratered, with domestic import premiums reaching record levels, underscoring a disconnect between futures positioning and physical demand.

Nvidia Halts H200 Shipments to China Amid Espionage Scandal Scrutiny

↓ Market Access

Nvidia confirmed it has stopped shipping licensed H200 chips to China after selling $2.4 billion worth of Hopper-generation products there last quarter, with interim CEO Colette Kress saying there are no current plans for further shipments. Roughly 600,000 H200 chips are reportedly stranded in Hong Kong awaiting Beijing approval that never came. A House Foreign Affairs Committee disclosure showed companies sought licenses to ship 3.4 million AI chips to China over six months, with only about 900,000 approved, amid heightened scrutiny following revelations about ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's alleged ties to a Chinese spy.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology
STEP 01 🔍

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

STEP 02 🔗

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

STEP 04 📬

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
