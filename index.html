Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariffs on Furniture and Lumber Take Effect

↑ Cost Pass-through

New Section 301 tariffs of up to 27% on products like Indonesian lauan plywood take effect October 14, layering a 19% reciprocal IEEPA tariff atop existing 8% MFN duties, while cabinet and vanity tariffs stay at 25% until 2027. Three lawsuits consolidated in the U.S. Court of International Trade argue the tariffs lack the country-specific unfair-trade-practice findings required for legal justification. The dispute adds cost uncertainty for import-reliant furniture and home-goods retailers heading into the holiday season.

Atlantic Hurricane Season Peak Threatens Gulf and Caribbean Ports

↓ Supply Disruption Risk

The 2026 Atlantic hurricane season's statistical peak runs from mid-August through mid-October, a window carrying initial Gulf Coast port closures of 24-72 hours and 5-10 days to full operational normalization after a direct hit. Caribbean transshipment hubs face feeder-service disruptions of 7-14 days, with emergency port surcharges of $50-300 per TEU applied when named storms threaten carrier rotations. The combined disruption window of 7-14 working days raises near-term freight cost and inventory risk for shippers routing through the region.

Congress Passes Stopgap Funding Through December 11, Averting Shutdown

↑ Stability

The bipartisan margin removes a near-term source of market and fiscal uncertainty heading into year-end appropriations talks.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology

Scan
