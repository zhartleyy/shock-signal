US Resumes Strikes on Iran, Brent Surges Toward $88

↑ Supply Shock

The US military launched fresh strikes on Iranian targets after Tehran attacked American assets, reigniting the months-long Strait of Hormuz crisis and pushing WTI up over 3% to $86.80/bbl and Brent to $87.93/bbl on August 1. Brent is on pace for a 20%-plus monthly gain as roughly 20-25% of global oil and LNG that normally transits Hormuz remains disrupted. Tanker traffic has partially recovered but stays well below the pre-war average of about 125 daily crossings.

Black Sea Oil DisruptionEnergy / Geopolitical

Fifth CPC Shutdown in a Month Cuts Kazakh Oil Output in Half

↓ Supply Deficit

Drone strikes hit the tankers NISSOS SIFNOS and MARATHI at the Novorossiysk terminal on July 30, the fifth suspension of Caspian Pipeline Consortium Black Sea loadings in a month. Kazakhstan's oil and condensate output has fallen from a June average of 2.16 million bpd to roughly 1 million bpd, with the giant Tengiz field cutting production by more than half. Five tankers bound for Novorossiysk redirected to Turkey and Spain, and the CPC route normally carries more than 80% of Kazakhstan's oil exports and nearly 2% of global supply.

Red Sea Blockade & CoalitionEnergy / Shipping / Geopolitical

Houthi Blockade Forces Saudi Arabia Into 50-Nation Maritime Coalition

↑ Cost Push

Since declaring a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia on July 20, Houthi forces have attacked Saudi-flagged tankers, including a missile strike on the NCC Ghazal, helping push Brent above $100 for the first time since May. With the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, Saudi Arabia had rerouted much of its crude through the Red Sea port of Yanbu, which the Houthi campaign now directly threatens. Riyadh convened nearly 50 countries to build a maritime defense coalition to protect the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, which normally carries about 12% of global trade.

Fed Hawkish HoldFinancial / Monetary

Fed Holds at 3.5%-3.75% for Fifth Straight Meeting, Three Officials Dissent

Rate Constraint

The FOMC voted 9-3 to hold its benchmark rate in a 3.5%-3.75% range, with Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari and Dallas's Lorie Logan all dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike, the first time since 2016 three officials have dissented in the same hawkish direction. Chair Kevin Warsh called the debate a 'family fight' and said the decision was a rigorous review rather than a pause. September hike odds rose to 59% on the CME FedWatch tool as officials cited inflation risk tied to the Middle East conflict, and the Dow fell more than 800 points that day, dragged by a 6.8% drop in Caterpillar.

Gold remained highly volatile in the wake of the Fed's hold decision, extending swings from the prior session as traders weighed hawkish dissents against a Middle-East-driven inflation shock. Trump Imposes 50% Tariff on Canadian Goods Effective Aug. 19

↑ Cost Push

Citing Section 338 of the Tariff Act for the first time, the administration unveiled tariffs covering Canadian alcoholic beverages and hockey equipment, dairy and confectionery goods, plus a broader basket spanning wood, electronics, plastics, furniture, machinery, apparel and autos-linked goods worth about $16 billion in 2026 trade. The tariffs take effect August 19, layering on top of existing Section 232 steel, aluminum and auto duties. The move raises input costs for US manufacturers reliant on integrated Canadian supply chains even as it shields some domestic producers from Canadian competition.

Section 301 Forced-Labor Tariff Hits Over 80 Trading Partners

↑ Cost Push

The US Trade Representative finalized a broad Section 301 tariff tied to forced-labor enforcement covering more than 80 trading partners on July 23, with rates ranging from 10% to 12.5% applied preferentially and roughly half of imports exempted; the measure is projected to raise $581 billion over 2026-2036. Separately, new tariffs on South Korean imports took effect August 1, lifting the blended effective US tariff rate to about 13.5% once steel, aluminum and universal duties are layered together, though J.P. Morgan left its Q3 GDP forecast unchanged. Korean exporters of autos and electronics face the most direct cost pressure from the combined duty stack.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
