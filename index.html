LNG Tanker Struck in Strait of Hormuz Reignites Chokepoint Risk

↑ Supply Risk Premium

An LNG tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, September 29, the latest in a string of attacks tied to the seven-month US-Israel war on Iran. The IRGC says it remains in an active military conflict over the strait, through which a large share of global oil and LNG normally transits. Tanker war-risk premiums remain elevated even as Gulf producers rely on armed escorts and 'dark' transits with AIS turned off to keep cargoes moving.

Gulf Oil Exports Rebound to 16.3 Million Barrels a Day Despite Ongoing Attacks

Mixed Signal

Middle East crude exports climbed to 16.328 million bpd in September, the highest since the war began in late February, after Saudi Arabia resumed tanker loadings from its Red Sea port of Yanbu via the restarted East-West Pipeline. Kpler data show total oil and product flows through Hormuz averaging 13.1 million bpd last week using military-escorted shuttles and rerouted exports. Yet Brent still rose 0.8% to $103.4 on Wednesday after Trump denied reports the US would ease Iran sanctions, showing the risk premium has not fully unwound even as physical supply normalizes.

US-China Detail $60B Tariff Cuts on Farm Goods, Coal and LNG

↑ Demand Normalization

The US and China detailed a plan to cut tariffs on roughly $30B of imports from each side, covering American farm products from corn to wheat and sorghum, coal, medical equipment and LNG, plus Chinese toys, kitchenware and household goods, following last week's Trump-Xi summit. Crop futures actually eased as traders await confirmed Chinese purchase signals rather than rallying on the announcement alone. The de-escalation also lowers the temperature on broader tech tensions, a tailwind for chipmakers with China exposure.

Canada Import Ban Takes Effect as Section 338 Tariffs Escalate

↓ Supply Constraint

A US ban on the importation of certain Canadian products took effect September 29, stacking on top of the existing 50% Section 338 tariff that applies regardless of USMCA status. Canada has matched with retaliatory tariffs covering $27.6 billion of US imports at rates of 15%, 25% or 50%. The escalating dispute is already hitting Canadian dairy farmers' US sales and threatens to disrupt auto and lumber supply chains reliant on cross-border inputs.

Trump's Global Section 301 Tariffs Face Fresh Trade Court Challenge

Regulatory Uncertainty

A three-judge panel at the US Court of International Trade heard arguments Wednesday from small businesses and Democratic-led states challenging Trump's sweeping Section 301 tariffs, which impose 10-12.5% duties on goods from 86 countries covering 99.4% of US imports. It is the third legal challenge to the administration's tariff authority in under two years, and a ruling against the White House could force retailers to unwind pricing built around the duties while stripping protection from tariff-shielded domestic industries.

Weak Jobs Data Cuts Fed Hike Odds, Gold Jumps to $4,185

↓ Rate Hike Odds

August JOLTS job openings printed at 7.079 million, well below the 7.23 million forecast, while consumer confidence data also slumped, cutting the odds of an October Fed rate hike to roughly 51% from about 71% a day earlier. Gold spot jumped 1.39% to $4,184.97 an ounce and silver rose 1.18% to $61.52, recovering part of Monday's steep losses, even as a two-month dollar high and 5.25% yields capped the rebound. The moves reflect an unusual environment where tariff- and war-driven inflation has kept the Fed biased toward tightening even as growth data softens.

Panama Canal Rolls Back Draft Restrictions as Rainfall Rebounds

↑ Supply Normalization

The Panama Canal Authority is rolling back transit and draft restrictions after rainfall returned to near-average levels, with Gatun Lake rising from 84.04 feet on September 1 to 84.74 feet on September 27. The move reverses a drought-driven bottleneck that had forced ships to reduce cargo loads and wait longer for transit slots, a disruption that had pushed some shippers toward longer routes or US rail alternatives.

Quietest Atlantic Hurricane Season in Years Eases Gulf Refinery Risk

↑ Supply Normalization

El Niño has suppressed Atlantic tropical activity to just five named storms through late September versus a nine-storm historical average, with zero hurricanes formed by mid-September against a typical four. That has kept the Gulf Coast's roughly 1.8 million bpd of offshore crude output and more than half of US refining capacity running without the shut-ins that periodically slash supply in active seasons, such as the roughly 300,000 bpd lost in September 2024.

How Catalyst Works

Methodology
STEP 01 🔍

Scan

Every morning, Catalyst scans global news for events creating supply or demand shocks — geopolitical, financial, regulatory, climatic and technological.

STEP 02 🔗

Trace

Each event is traced through its causal chain: what becomes scarcer or more abundant, and which companies sit directly in the path of that change?

STEP 03 🎯

Score

A conviction score (1–10) is assigned on directness of the causal link, revenue exposure, and how much of the effect is already priced into the stock.

STEP 04 📬

Deliver

The full brief is published each morning with winners, losers, macro themes and double beneficiaries — ready before markets open.
