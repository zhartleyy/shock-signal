Hormuz FlashpointEnergy / Geopolitical

Iran Mines Tankers Near Hormuz, Oil Risk Premium Resurges

↑ Supply Risk Premium

Iran's Revolutionary Guards reported multiple tankers and a tugboat struck mines or caught fire in and near the Strait of Hormuz on Nov 5-6, with UKMTO also logging a tanker hit by projectiles off Qatar. The strait, which normally carries about a fifth of global oil consumption, is running at roughly 2 vessel transits a day versus an 85-per-day pre-crisis baseline. Brent whipsawed from an 8% plunge to $94.62 on hopes of US-Iran diplomacy back toward the $99-100 range as attacks resumed after the US midterms.

Hormuz FlashpointShipping / Energy

VLCC Freight Rates Rewrite Oil Economics

↑ Supply Constraint

Freight has become a bigger swing factor in delivered crude prices than the headline barrel: shipping a US Gulf Coast-to-Asia VLCC cargo now costs roughly $30-35 per barrel versus about $5 in 2025, with a single voyage running $60-65 million. A standard 2-million-barrel cargo now carries more than $74 million in freight versus under $15 million before the conflict. Clarksons has roughly doubled its 2026 average VLCC rate forecast to about $135,000 per day from an original $75,000 estimate, with some Middle East-to-Asia routes recently topping $750,000-$1 million+ per day.

OPEC+ and US LNG Fill the Gulf Supply Gap

Mixed Signal

With Hormuz-linked crude flows unreliable, Gulf producers and non-Middle East suppliers are capturing share as buyers prioritize security of supply over marginal cost. The IEA has previously called the broader episode one of the largest energy security crises on record, underscoring sustained structural reallocation of trade flows away from the strait.

National Data Center Moratorium Calls Spark $2 Trillion Tech Rout

↓ Demand Destruction

Progressive lawmakers, including Bernie Sanders, called for a national data center moratorium after the midterms, intensifying a selloff that wiped out roughly $2 trillion in tech market value and dropped the Nasdaq more than 5%. AI Power Demand Still Surges Despite Policy Headwinds

Mixed Signal

That dynamic creates a bifurcated trade: power providers benefit while pure-play neocloud operators absorb the regulatory risk.

Typhoon Champi Triggers Fourth Port Disruption in Seven Weeks

↑ Supply Chain Delay

Typhoon Champi, the fourth typhoon to strike East Asian port networks in seven weeks, pushed Shanghai ship waiting times to about 3.4 days versus a global average of 1.3 days in the latest reported week. Port Klang yard density is running at 85-90% with 1.5-2 day waits, while Jakarta faces 2-3 day berth queues, and analysts warn recovery can take five weeks in quiet periods and up to two months at peak. The cumulative disruption raises the risk that delays roll into the pre-Lunar New Year shipping rush, tightening vessel and container availability globally.
