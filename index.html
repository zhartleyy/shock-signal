VLCC Freight Hits Record $1.3 Million a Day

↑ Freight Cost Spike

Tanker freight out of the Persian Gulf has added roughly $33 a barrel to shipping costs, up from just $1.73 in January, as owners demand compensation for war-zone transits. The surge pushed Brent's premium over WTI to about $13.80 a barrel on October 6, with VLCC day rates reaching a record $1.3 million. WTI, priced inland at Cushing, has been largely insulated from the waterborne cost spike, widening the spread between the two benchmarks.

Asian Port Congestion Ties Up 8.5% of Container Fleet

↑ Freight Rate Spike

Repeated typhoons have compounded operational bottlenecks across major Asian container ports, sidelining about 8.5% of the global containership fleet and raising the risk the capacity crunch extends into 2027. Drewry's Intra-Asia Container Index has hit record levels, with Shanghai-Laem Chabang rates up 22% to $1,609 per 40-foot container and Shanghai-Jakarta rates up 12% to $2,300 as China's Golden Week demand collided with constrained vessel capacity. Analysts warn the disruption will take months rather than weeks to unwind.

Silver's Sixth Straight Supply Deficit Underpins $61 Price Floor

↓ Supply Deficit

The Silver Institute and Metals Focus project a 2026 global silver deficit of 46.3 million ounces, a sixth consecutive annual shortfall, with the cumulative gap since 2021 reaching nearly 762 million ounces against total demand of 1,112.6 million ounces. Silver traded near $61/oz on October 6, more than double early-2025 levels despite retreating sharply from January's record $121.62 high. Declining ore grades and limited new mine supply in Mexico, Peru and China keep the structural deficit intact.

US-China Truce Extended to January 2027, Tariffs Cut $30 Billion

↑ Demand Normalization

Treasury Secretary Bessent extended the US-China trade truce two months to January 10, 2027, with USTR Jamieson Greer saying export controls that are 'national security issues' were taken off the table, alongside a $30 billion tariff cut and a new AI dialogue. Chip tensions persist regardless: on October 1 prosecutors charged a California man with smuggling more than $300 million of Nvidia servers to China via Singapore and Malaysia, and officials believe China has obtained compute equal to over 115,000 restricted Nvidia chips. Tencent is also reportedly leasing roughly 100,000 AI chips through Oracle data centers in Southeast Asia to route around the curbs entirely.

China's Rare-Earth Export Pause Set to Expire November 2026

↓ Supply Deficit Risk

China's suspension of its most extreme October-2025 rare-earth controls, covering holmium, erbium, thulium, europium and ytterbium plus a foreign direct product rule triggered by as little as 0.1% Chinese-origin content, is due to lapse around November 10, 2026. The original April-2025 controls on seven other rare earths, including dysprosium, terbium and scandium, remain fully active with a 45-day licensing process that continues to delay shipments. The restriction framework already covers 12 of 17 recognized rare earth elements, creating simultaneous supply risk across magnets, EVs, smartphones and defense platforms.

OPEC+ Holds November Output Flat for Second Month

↑ Price Support

Seven core OPEC+ members, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman, agreed at an October 4 virtual meeting to maintain September's required production levels through November, the second consecutive month without an increase. OECD commercial oil inventories fell 26.4 million barrels in June to 2.73 billion barrels, leaving stocks 66.5 million barrels below the five-year average. Actual output remains well below official quotas for several members as the Iran war continues to constrain exports and production capacity.

STEP 03 🎯
