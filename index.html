Middle East De-escalationGeopolitical / Energy

US–Iran Peace Deal Text Finalized — Strait of Hormuz Set to Reopen

US and Iranian negotiators, with Pakistani mediation, agreed the final text of a draft peace deal on June 12, addressing an end to hostilities, sanctions relief, and maritime access through the Strait of Hormuz. Trump signaled an imminent signing in Europe "within days." The strait — which carries ~25% of seaborne oil and 20% of LNG — had been effectively closed since the conflict began in late February. Reopening unwinds the largest supply shock of the cycle: the demand for "scarcity premium" energy collapses while transport-cost relief flows to consumers and industrials. Nuclear issues remain a separate follow-on track, leaving some headline risk.

Oil Tumbles ~20% from 2026 Peak — Brent Near $88, Lowest in Two Months

Middle East De-escalationShipping / LNG

LNG & Tanker Rates Set to Collapse as Cape Rerouting Ends

With Hormuz reopening, the multiplier behind sky-high freight — Cape of Good Hope rerouting that added 3,500–4,000 nautical miles and 10–14 days per voyage — unwinds. The same fleet recovers 15–20% of effective capacity overnight as voyages shorten, deflating the VLCC and LNG charter rates that had spiked to extraordinary levels. Qatar LNG volumes return to market, easing the spot premium that US Gulf Coast exporters had been commanding. This is a direct reversal of the shipping-scarcity and LNG-vacuum theses that dominated the prior cycle.

Hawkish Central BanksMacro / Europe

ECB Delivers First Rate Hike Since 2023 — Key Rate to 2.25%

The ECB raised its key rate a quarter point to 2.25% on June 11 — its first increase since 2023 — to counter inflation pushed to 3.2% in May by the Iran-war energy shock. The hawkish pivot strengthens the euro versus the dollar and lifts net interest income for European banks, while raising the discount rate on rate-sensitive European equities. With the energy shock now reversing (Signals 1–3), the ECB may be tightening into peak inflation, but the immediate beneficiary is the European banking complex.

USTR Proposes 12.5% Section 301 Tariffs on 60 Economies; 50% Metals Duties Stand

After the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs in February, the USTR pivoted to Section 301, proposing up to 12.5% tariffs on 60 economies (59 countries plus the EU) over forced-labor enforcement, with comments due July 6 and hearings July 7. Separately, a June 1 proclamation holds Section 232 duties on steel, aluminum, and copper derivatives at 50%. The mechanism is a supply-side cost wall: domestic metals producers gain pricing protection while import-heavy consumer and apparel names face margin compression they cannot fully pass through.

Taiwan and US Tighten AI Chip Export Controls Targeting China

Taiwan is weighing much stricter export controls on AI chip sales to China to align with Washington and curb smuggling of Nvidia-based AI servers, while the US Commerce Department clarified that licensing requirements extend to subsidiaries of Chinese firms located outside China. The mechanism is a regulatory supply constraint on China-bound advanced silicon: it entrenches the moat of compliant Western suppliers but caps China-revenue exposure for equipment makers and creates urgency for domestic Chinese substitution. Nvidia notes it already operates within the clarified rules.

European Rearmament Runs Ahead of Schedule Amid Structural Copper Deficit

European defense spending grew 14% in 2025 to €739B — the steepest climb since the 1950s — with Germany's budget heading toward €117B in 2026 and France at €68.5B, as NATO targets rise. Goldman projects this lifts European industrial-metals demand ~6% by 2027. That collides with a structural copper supply deficit of ~330K tonnes in 2026 (prices near $12,100/t) driven by concentrate shortages and thin new mine supply. The mechanism stacks defense and electrification demand onto a constrained metals supply curve, while sustaining multi-year defense order books.
