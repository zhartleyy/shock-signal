Middle East Energy ShockShipping / Insurance

War-Risk Insurance and VLCC Freight Rates Re-Price to Record Levels

↑ Freight Rate Surge

Marine war-risk premiums for transiting the Persian Gulf have been repriced sharply higher, with insurers citing 9-15 damaged tankers since the conflict began and per-voyage war-risk costs running into the hundreds of thousands of dollars. VLCC benchmark rates for hauling Middle East crude to China hit an all-time high above $423,000 per day earlier in the conflict, and diversion around the Cape of Good Hope adds 3,500-4,000 nautical miles and 10-14 days per voyage. This structurally reduces effective global tanker capacity even though nominal fleet size is unchanged, supporting day-rates for owners with uncommitted spot tonnage.

Gold Slides Toward 9-Month Low Despite Active Middle East War

↓ Safe-Haven Demand

Gold fell to around $4,000/oz, near a nine-month low, even as the US-Iran war escalated to a 10th day of strikes and Houthis declared a new maritime embargo - a rare case of a geopolitical shock failing to lift the safe-haven metal. The disconnect stems from oil-driven inflation forcing markets to price out cuts and price in hikes instead, strengthening the dollar and lifting real yields, both headwinds for non-yielding bullion. Gold ETFs saw roughly $2 billion of outflows in May alone, the first monthly Asian-fund outflow since August 2025, compounding pressure from the hawkish Fed repricing.

Brazil's 25% Tariff Takes Effect July 22 With Carve-Outs for Oil, Beef, Coffee

↑ Trade Friction

A 25% US tariff on Brazilian goods takes effect July 22 following a Section 301 investigation, though a nearly 100-page exemption list spares oil and gas, beef, coffee, and oranges to avoid domestic supply disruption. USTR Jamieson Greer cited issues from digital trade to market access, while the move is described as a prelude to a broader rollout that could cover more than 80 countries within weeks. Brazilian President Lula called the tariffs a "lamentable milestone" given the US trade surplus with Brazil.

Section 122 Global Tariff Set to Expire July 24, Section 301 Successor Looms

Regulatory Transition Risk

The temporary 10% Section 122 global tariff, imposed after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA-based tariffs, is scheduled to expire July 24, 2026, with the administration proposing a 10-12.5% Section 301 forced-labor tariff on goods from 60 countries as its replacement. Standard MFN, Section 232, and Section 301 duties will begin applying to postal shipments for the first time as the de minimis threshold rises from $800 to $2,500. Importers are racing to clear goods before July 24 to lock in the lower current rate rather than the potentially higher successor tariff.
